Module 02 — Fundamentals

What a
wholesale
actually is

You cannot explain this to an agent if you don't understand it yourself. Most people who get in trouble get in trouble here.

The one idea underneath everything

When we sign a purchase agreement with a seller, we get something real. It's called an equitable interest — a contractual right to buy that property at an agreed price.

We don't own the house. We own the contract.

And here's the part that matters: the contract is ours to sell. That's our own property, our own position, our own asset. Selling something we own is not brokerage. That is the entire legal foundation of what you do.

Say this to yourself before every call

"I am not selling their house. I am selling our contract."

The four exits — know all four cold

ExitWhat happensWhat you say on the phone
Assignment We hand our contract to another investor for a fee. They close with the seller. We never take title. "We're looking to wholesale the transaction."
Double close We close with the seller, then immediately resell to the investor. Two closings, same day or days apart. "We're looking to wholesale the transaction." Same phrase — the mechanics are our business, not the agent's.
Wholetail We close on it ourselves, do light work — haul the junk, paint, cut the grass, clean it up — then put it right back on the market. No full renovation. "We may close on it and put it back on the market — clean it up a little, cut the grass, and list it."
Full flip We close, renovate properly, then list it. Longest timeline, biggest spread. "We may close on it, fix it up, and list it with an agent."

Wholetail is the one people get confused about. It is not a flip and it is not a wholesale. It's the middle path: we take title, spend a small amount to make it presentable, and sell it retail. It matters to your call because it's a genuine reason an agent might get a listing out of you.

Two numbers, two different worlds

This is the distinction that keeps you out of trouble. There are two completely different prices in any conversation, and you are allowed to talk about exactly one of them.

 The numberCan you say it?
OURS What Equity Track wants for its own contract position. "If we wholesale the transaction we'd want at least the number in the file." YES — it's our own asset and our own asking price. Read it off the file. Never from memory.
THEIRS What the property should be listed at, what it will appraise for, what a buyer should offer, what the ARV is. NO — that is licensed advice. That is the agent's job. That is where you hand it off.

Where the trouble comes from

Not from naming our price. From acting like a salesman holding prices — going back and forth, defending a valuation, telling an agent what the market will bear, negotiating a retail sale. That is an agent's work, and doing it without a license is the thing that ends careers and costs companies money.

You state our number once, from the file. Then you stop. Anything after that goes to Bryan.

Module 03 — Boundaries

Your lane —
and the fence
around it

Who you are: a dispositions rep at an investment company. Not an agent. Not a broker. Not a negotiator for anybody but us.

Say it before they ask

The moment price, value, or listing comes up, you get ahead of it. Out loud, in your own words, every single time:

The disclosure — this is the most protective sentence you own

"Quick disclosure here — I'm not a real estate agent. I'm not trying to be a real estate agent. My company either wants to find someone to list the property, or we'll put it right back on the market ourselves."

Say it early and say it relaxed. It costs you nothing and it removes the single biggest reason an agent would ever have a problem with your call.

The fence

 What you do

YOU CANSay we have the property under contract and it's off market.
YOU CANSay we're either wholesaling the transaction, or closing and putting it back on the market.
YOU CANSay title is already open, and where.
YOU CANState what our company wants for the transaction — read from the file.
YOU CANSay we're looking for an agent to list it, and that their brokerage gets paid through escrow.
YOU CANAsk what their investor clients buy, where, at what price, and how fast.
YOU CANSend the property file, photos, and address.
NEVERSay or imply you're an agent, broker, or Realtor — or that you're "representing" anyone.
NEVERSay we own the property when we don't.

NEVERNegotiate a retail sale, or go back and forth defending a price. State ours once, then stop.

The two-second rule for anything unusual

If a call turns into something you weren't trained for — someone asking about the company itself, about a mailer or a campaign, about a dispute, about a lawyer, or anyone calling from an agency or a government office — you say one thing:

"I'm not the right person for that. Let me get you to the right person and have them call you back today."

Then you take a name and a number, say nothing else, answer no questions, promise nothing, and you get it to Juan immediately. Not tomorrow. Immediately. You will never be in trouble here for routing something up. You can absolutely be in trouble for improvising.

Three phrases that end your call safely

"I'll get you the file."

Covers every factual question you can't answer — condition, cost, size, permits, comps.

"That's a question for our team, I'll get an answer today."

Covers price movement, terms, timelines, anything that smells like negotiation.

"I'm not a real estate agent, so that part's yours."

Covers list price, market strategy, and anything requiring a license.

Module 04 — The script

The call,
word for word

This is the script. Not a starting point, not an inspiration — the script. Learn it until it sounds like you, then don't wander off it.

Before you dial you know one thing: this agent sold something near our property. That's why you're calling and you say it out loud.

The opener

"Hello — my name is Danny, I'm with Equity Track. I have a property in Oakland and wanted to see if you had a few minutes to talk."

"Yeah, go ahead."

"I saw you did the one around the corner."

"Yep, that was mine."

"Okay, great — thank you. So I have this property. It's under contract, it's off market. We're either going to close on it and put it back on the market — we're thinking maybe a wholetail, where we go in, clean it up a little, cut the grass, and list it right back on the market — or we're going to wholesale the transaction. Title is already open, it's open at CWFG. And that's pretty much it."

— then stop talking —

Why every piece of that is in there

LineJob it does
"A few minutes to talk"Asks permission. An agent who says yes has agreed to the conversation instead of enduring it.
"I saw you did the one around the corner"Proves you did homework. This is the whole difference between you and the spam they delete.
"Under contract, off market"Honest position, stated before they have to ask. You're a real buyer with a real contract.
"Close on it and put it back on the market"A possible listing for them. This is a reason to like you.
"Or wholesale the transaction"A possible deal for their investor buyer. Second reason to like you.
"Title is already open at CWFG"The credibility line. Tire-kickers don't have title open. This says we're actually doing this.
"That's pretty much it"Hands them the floor. Do not fill this silence.

When they ask the price question

They will. "What are you asking for it?" Here is the full answer, and notice the order — disclosure first, number second, handoff third.

The price answer

"What are you asking for it?"

"So — quick disclosure here. I'm not a real estate agent. I'm not trying to be a real estate agent. My company is either looking for someone to list the property, or we'll put it right back on the market ourselves.

If we wholesale the transaction, we'd want at least the number in the file. And on pricing for what the property should actually sell for — that's where you'd come in. That's exactly the kind of agent we're looking for to step in."

Read the wholesale number off the file every time. Never from memory, never rounded, never "somewhere around." One number, once, from the file. Then you're done talking about price.

And notice what that last sentence does: it takes the thing you're not allowed to do — set a retail price — and turns it into the reason you want them on the deal. You didn't dodge. You promoted them.

Do not sound like a script

Read this out loud twenty times until the words stop being words on a screen. Then close the screen and talk like a person. If an agent can hear you reading, the call is already over.

The handoff

Close every good call like this

"Perfect — I'll send you the address and the photos in the next ten minutes. What's the best email? And while I've got you: what are your investor clients actually looking for right now?"

Module 05 — Objections

When they
push back

Every real agent tests you. Most of the tests are the same six questions, and every one has a clean answer that keeps you in your lane.

"Do you own the house? What's going on with it?"

Answer straight and fast

"No — of course. I'm with Equity Track, we're an investment company. We've got it under contract, off market. We're either going to wholesale the transaction, or close on it, clean it up a little, and put it right back on the market. Title's already open at CWFG."

Why "no, of course" works: it's honest, it's relaxed, and it tells the agent this is a normal question you field all day. Hesitate here and they decide you're hiding something.

A "no" is only a failure if you hang up empty

An agent who passes on the property but tells you their buyer wants Alameda duplexes, $700K to $1M, cosmetic only, cash in 12 days — that agent just paid you. Six months from now an Alameda duplex lands and you know exactly who to call.

Module 06 — The record

If it's not
written down,
it didn't happen

Your memory is not the database. Every conversation ends with these fields filled in.

The four questions that fill it

  1. "Where are your investor clients buying?"
  2. "What's their price range, and how much work will they take on?"
  3. "How fast can they close, and is it cash?"
  4. "What's something they're having trouble finding right now?"

That fourth question is the whole job. When an agent tells you their buyer desperately needs another Oakland duplex, you just handed acquisitions a shopping list. We stop selling houses and start matching inventory to demand.

Your daily floor

  • 20 contact attempts a day
  • 8 real conversations logged a day
  • 5 new buy boxes a week
  • 15 net-new people contacted before a property goes to our usual buyers

Practicing with AI — read this part carefully

The drill block uses AI as a sparring partner and it works. But be clear on what it is: a practice opponent, not a source of facts.

Use it for

Reps. Tone. Getting the opener smooth. Running the price answer thirty times without burning a real agent.

Never use it for

Anything you'd repeat as fact — prices, comps, ARVs, rehab costs, permit status, market statistics. Models invent numbers with total confidence.

If the practice partner gives you a comp or an ARV, it made it up. Ignore it and keep drilling the conversation. Every real number you say out loud comes off the property file.

No property today? Then it's a network day.

Zero inventory does not mean zero work. All four dialing hours go to relationship calls with no specific address attached — which are also the calls with no contract requirement. Build Thursday's buyer three months early.
